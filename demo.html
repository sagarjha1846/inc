Common questions

Why can’t ChatGPT see my site?

Almost always one of three things. Your robots.txt disallows the crawler that fetches pages to answer questions — often copied from a blog post about "blocking AI scrapers", without realising the fetchers and the training crawlers are different. Or the page renders client-side, so what the server sends is an empty <div id="root"> and the crawler, which does not run JavaScript, reads a blank page. Or nothing on the page says machine-readably who published it, so the engine has no confident attribution to show and quotes a competitor instead.

Which crawlers actually decide whether I get cited?

Not the ones most people block. Citations come from the search and browsing agents: OAI-SearchBot and ChatGPT-User (ChatGPT), Claude-SearchBot and Claude-User (Claude), PerplexityBot and Perplexity-User. Blocking any of those removes you from that engine’s answers entirely, no matter how good the page is. The training crawlers — GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, CCBot — read pages to build models, not to answer today’s question.

Is blocking AI crawlers a mistake?

Blocking the training crawlers is a legitimate content policy, and plenty of publishers make that choice deliberately. Blocking the citation crawlers is almost always an accident, because the two get bundled into the same copied snippet. They are separate decisions and this audit scores them separately — you can refuse to train the models and still want to be the source they cite.

What should my robots.txt say?

If you want citations and are relaxed about training, the shortest honest version is to allow everything. If you want citations but not training, name the crawlers: allow OAI-SearchBot, Claude-SearchBot and PerplexityBot, disallow GPTBot, and declare your sitemap. Rules are matched by longest path, not by file order, and a Disallow: / under User-agent: * applies to every crawler that has no group of its own.

User-agent: OAI-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: Claude-SearchBot
Allow: /

User-agent: PerplexityBot
Allow: /

User-agent: GPTBot
Disallow: /

Sitemap: https://yoursite.com/sitemap.xml

Does llms.txt matter yet?

Honestly, it is unsettled. /llms.txt is a plain-Markdown index that tells a model what your site is and which pages matter, and adoption is early enough that it may become a standard or may fade. It costs one file, so this audit weights it lightly — 2 points out of 100 — rather than pretending the question is decided. Nothing else in the score depends on it.

How long does any of this take to fix?

The crawler-access fix is a few lines of robots.txt and takes effect the next time a crawler visits. Structured data is one <script type="application/ld+json"> block in the head. Server-rendering a client-rendered page is the expensive one, and it is the only finding here that is a project rather than an afternoon.

What gets checked

Crawler access

Whether robots.txt lets the crawlers that produce citations reach the page at all — checked per crawler, per path.

Readable content

Whether the answer exists in the HTML. Most AI crawlers do not run JavaScript, so a client-rendered page reads as blank.

Answer structure

Whether a model can lift a clean answer: one H1, question-shaped headings, lists and tables, a direct opening paragraph.

Structured data

Whether the page states machine-readable facts about who published it and what kind of content it is.

Metadata

Title, description, canonical, language and Open Graph — the text engines reuse when they build a source card.

Authority

Authorship, freshness dates and outbound citations, which decide who wins between two sources making the same claim.
